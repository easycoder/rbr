Comfort, with control

Take control of your heating, room by room.

Room By Room Heating brings together commercially available ZigBee devices, a compact low-cost mini-PC and custom software. The aim is simple: heat the rooms that are in use, rather than heating the whole home unnecessarily.

That is where the saving comes from. A spare room, a study, a child’s bedroom after the school run: rooms nobody is using can sit at a lower temperature, without anyone having to remember to turn a radiator down and then up again. How much a household saves depends on how closely it manages its heating today, and the least-managed homes have the most to gain.

And the system is reached through a web page on a phone: every room, its temperature and its schedule, whether you are in the house or away. The plan itself runs at home, so the heating carries on whether or not the app is open.

How Room By Room Heating works

Smart devices work together to help manage warmth in the rooms you use.

Built from readily available technology

Smart control, without a complicated-looking system.

The system is based on commercially available ZigBee devices and a small mini-PC running dedicated software. What the visit involves depends on the house and on the route chosen for its radiators, but it is fitting and wiring rather than building work.

The devices

A controller the size of a paperback beside the router, a small radio dongle on a lead, a battery thermometer in each room, a valve head at each radiator — powered by a relay, or self-contained with no cabling — and one relay at the boiler asking it for heat. The boiler, the pipework and the radiators themselves are unchanged.

What the house needs

A working heating system, with a boiler or heat pump that can be told when heat is wanted; a mains socket where the controller will sit; a wired connection to the router, so a spare port and a cable route; and access to each radiator that is to be controlled. The household’s Wi-Fi is not used and the router is not reconfigured. The only thing a household has to supply is its room names.

Who does the work

The unit is paired, named and configured before it arrives, so the visit is fitting. Replacing standard valve heads is ordinary heating work. New mains wiring — a supply at the boiler’s demand input, or at radiator relays on the powered route — is a job for a qualified electrician, and work on a gas boiler’s controls is a job for a Gas Safe-registered heating engineer. Where a valve body has to be changed or an adapter fitted, that is a plumber’s or heating engineer’s job.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Does it heat the whole house?

The aim is to manage heating room by room, so settings can reflect which rooms are being used. The exact behaviour depends on the installed devices and system configuration.

Does it use specialist equipment?

The concept is based on commercially available ZigBee devices, a low-cost mini-PC and custom software. Compatibility should be confirmed for the equipment used in each installation.

Will it work with my existing heating system?

That depends on your boiler, radiators, valves and controls. A suitability check is needed before making any promises about compatibility.

What happens if the broadband goes down?

The heating carries on. The plan and the decisions live in the controller at home, so each room keeps following its schedule; it is only the app that cannot reach the system until the connection returns.

Will it save me money?

It can, and the saving comes from not heating rooms nobody is using. Its size depends far more on the household than on the system: a home that already turns its radiators down has little to gain, while one that leaves empty rooms warm through the day has more. We would not quote a percentage for every house.

What does a system cost?

It is the parts plus a trade’s time to fit them, and it scales with the number of rooms and radiators rather than the size of the house. The parts are ordinary items — a small computer, a thermometer or valve head per room, a relay per radiator and one at the boiler — so they can be quoted rather than guessed, and nothing is bought again each year.

What happens if a room sensor stops reporting?

After about three quarters of an hour without a reading, that room is treated as out of date: the system stops calling for heat there and holds the radiator off until the reading returns, while the app marks the last known temperature as stale. The room cools gently rather than being heated blind.

What is a PWA?

The web app is a progressive web app (PWA): a web page that can be added to a phone’s home screen and used like an installed app, with nothing to download from a store. Opened without a system paired to it, it shows a working demonstration.

Open source

For DIY installers and the technically curious, the controller software, the app and the documentation are public on GitHub. Room By Room is an open-source project — read how it works, or build and run it yourself.
