# Deployment and roles

> **Audience:** industry — specifiers, heating contractors, developers, housing providers · **Status:** draft · **Last verified:** 2026-09-21 · **See also:** [The experience is the product](00-the-experience-is-the-product.md) · [A fitted house](03-a-fitted-house.md) · [Costs and payback](05-costs-and-payback.md) · [Glossary](../appendix/glossary.md)

## Five jobs, however they are shared

For a system to exist and keep working, five things have to be done. They can be spread across a supplier, an installer and a household, or collapsed into a single firm; in a small job they usually come down to two people, whoever prepares the unit and whoever fits it. What matters is not who they are but that each one is somebody's job.

**Preparing the unit.** Before it is delivered, the devices are paired and named, each is bound to its room, the plan and its schedules are built, and the controller's address is recorded. This is the work that makes everything afterwards mechanical, and it can be done by the supplier of the unit, by the installer, or in-house by a provider.

**Fitting it.** On site, the valves, relays and thermometers are fitted and wired, and the controller is connected to the router. This is ordinary heating work, deliberately nothing more: no network to design, no software to set up, nothing to decide.

**Commissioning it.** A final check that it does what was intended — each room reads, each relay switches, the boiler answers, and the plan is the one that was agreed.

**Operating it.** The times, the temperatures and the exceptions, week in and week out. A household, or a provider's staff. In a managed property it may be nobody at all from the resident's side.

**Standing behind it.** Somebody to call. The systems of the first wave left this job empty, and the rest of RBR is shaped to keep it filled and cheap: faults surface where they can be seen remotely, and the parts are ordinary trade items, so the person who answers can be the trade that fitted it.

## A single home

The household decides to have one, an installer fits it, and the household runs it. What the household actually does is small: the rooms and their times are settled once, with the installer, and after that the app is opened only for exceptions.

The support line is short and personal — the installer who fitted it, or the supplier who prepared it. In many jobs that is the same business, which is a feature rather than a limitation: it is one call, to somebody who knows the house.

## A provider's stock

A housing provider's estate runs the same system with the roles separated and assigned.

The units are prepared to a standard pattern: a repeat house type has a known set of rooms and a known set of schedules, so preparing one unit is very nearly preparing the next. A contractor fits and wires them. The provider's staff keep every installation together and work them centrally, so the operating and support jobs sit with the provider rather than with the household. What that looks like from the outside is described in [A fitted house](03-a-fitted-house.md).

## Where each job lands

| Job | A single home | A provider's stock |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare the unit | The supplier, or the installer | The provider or their contractor, to a standard pattern |
| Fit and wire it | The installer | The provider's contractor |
| Commission it | The installer | The contractor, checked against the provider's standard |
| Set the rooms and schedules | The household, with the installer | The provider, or the household by agreement |
| Operate it day to day | The household | The household, or the provider's staff |
| Stand behind it | The installer or supplier | The provider's staff, with the supplier behind them |
| Replace a failed part | The trade that fitted the system | The same trade, arranged by the provider |

## The one thing the household has to supply

The room names, and what each room is for — gathered before the unit is prepared, because they are what the devices are named after and what the plan is built from. Everything else can be decided and set up without the household being involved at all; the reasons are in [A fitted house](03-a-fitted-house.md).
