# A fitted house

> **Audience:** industry — specifiers, heating contractors, developers, housing providers · **Status:** draft · **Last verified:** 2026-09-20 · **See also:** [The experience is the product](00-the-experience-is-the-product.md) · [What RBR is](02-what-rbr-is.md) · [Deployment and roles](04-deployment-and-roles.md) · [Glossary](../appendix/glossary.md)

## What the household notices

Very little, most of the time — which is the point. The radiators are the same radiators and the boiler is the same boiler. What changes is that the house stops being one zone and becomes a set of rooms, each with its own temperature and its own timetable, without anyone having to think about it.

If there was a thermostat in the hall, it is no longer the thing that decides for the whole house. It can stay as a manual control, but it is no longer the brain.

## A room at a time

The same equipment does quite different work depending on the room.

- A kitchen or living room used through the day can be held warm from morning to night.
- Bedrooms can be warm at the ends of the day and cool overnight, when nobody is in them.
- A spare room can be left off altogether, and warmed only when a visitor is expected.
- A bathroom can take short bursts, at the times the household actually uses it.

Each of these is a schedule, set once and then left. No room's timetable constrains another's.

## What the boiler sees

RBR does not run the house as a single zone. Each room decides for itself, and the boiler is asked for heat only while at least one room wants it. A house with two rooms in use on a winter evening runs the boiler for those two rooms, not for every radiator in the property; when the last room's demand is met, the boiler is released.

## How a house gets one

There are two ways a house comes by RBR, and they are worth separating.

**Fitted to an existing home.** The devices can be paired and named, and the rooms and schedules set up, before the unit reaches the house; the visit is then fitting and wiring, plus connecting the controller to the router.

**Built into a new one.** Here the point of the design shows. A repeat house type has a known set of rooms, so the configuration for one house is very nearly the configuration for the next. Units are paired, named and prepared before delivery, and what reaches the site is an appliance to be installed rather than a design to be settled.

Either way, one small thing has to come from the household first: the room names. A room-by-room system needs the room count in any case; giving them their names is the natural next step, and it is what the devices are named after and what the plan is built from. So they are gathered in advance. The system is given its address before delivery too, so the app can be pointed at the house straight away.

## Buildings and estates

The same system scales from a single house to a block of flats or a housing provider's whole stock without becoming a different product.

A provider can group its installations, keep them in one place, and see all of them from a phone; faults can often be spotted, and cleared, before a resident has to report anything. A resident who would rather not think about the heating at all can be left out of it entirely, or given a simple view of their own home and nothing else. Who does what is the subject of the next document.

## Where the equipment goes

Almost all of it is out of sight. What the parts are is described in [What RBR is](02-what-rbr-is.md); this is where they live in a property.

- A small computer near the router, and a small plug-in radio that talks to the room devices. Together these are the only new equipment in the household's living space, and both can sit on a shelf.
- In each room, a temperature sensor — a small device on a wall or a shelf, reading its own room rather than a hallway, or built into the radiator's valve instead.
- At each radiator, a valve the system opens and closes: either a powered valve switched by a relay, or a self-contained one that senses and switches by itself and needs no wiring. Where a room has more than one heat emitter — a radiator and a plinth heater, say — each is switched in its own right.
- At the boiler, one relay on its demand input.

How those parts are wired belongs to the installation section rather than here.
